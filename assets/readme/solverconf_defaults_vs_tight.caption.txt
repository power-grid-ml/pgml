What the default settings cost. Every grid is solved four times on a model proven identical in pgml and in the reference tool: by both at tight settings (double precision, tolerance 1e-12) and by both at their default settings. One marker per grid; the five panels are five measures of the same comparison: $\max_i\,|\Delta \underline{V}_i|$ (pu), the largest difference of any complex node voltage in per unit; $\max_i\,|\Delta |\underline{V}_i||$ (pu), the largest difference of any node voltage magnitude in per unit; $\max_i\,|\Delta \theta_i|$ (degrees), the largest difference of any node voltage angle in degrees (nodes above 1e-6 pu only); $\max_b\,|\Delta I_b|\,/\,\max_b I_b$, the largest difference of any branch current magnitude, relative to the largest branch current of the grid; $|\Delta P_\mathrm{loss}|\,/\,P_\mathrm{loss}$, the difference of the total series losses, relative to the losses. The dashed line with the shaded region below it is the largest deviation over all grids when both sides use tight settings, the best agreement the two implementations can reach. Crosses: the tool at its defaults against pgml's tight solve. Circles: pgml at its defaults against the tool's tight solve. A marker far above the dashed line means that the default settings of that side cause the deviation, not its mathematics. Largest complex voltage deviation at defaults: pandapower 4.5e-9 pu (tool), 2.2e-10 pu (pgml); power-grid-model 1.7e-12 pu (tool), 2.2e-10 pu (pgml); OpenDSS 1.7e-5 pu (tool), 1.3e-9 pu (pgml). Defaults compared: pgml tolerance 1e-8, pandapower tolerance_mva 1e-8, power-grid-model error_tolerance 1e-8, OpenDSS Tolerance 1e-4.
